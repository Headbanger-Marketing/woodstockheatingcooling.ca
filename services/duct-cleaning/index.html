Duct Cleaning

When is duct cleaning appropriate, and how can a restriction be distinguished from damaged ductwork?

Duct cleaning is a targeted service, not a routine interval to assume for every property. The request should identify what was observed, such as visible debris at a register or material found during work on the duct system. Weak airflow alone does not establish that ducts need cleaning; a crushed section, closed damper, filter fit, or other restriction may be responsible. Inspection helps distinguish removable buildup from a duct defect that requires a different remedy.

Assessment should establish where debris is visible, which runs are affected, and whether airflow complaints occur at particular registers. Inspection can distinguish loose buildup from a crushed duct, closed damper, or another obstruction. The condition and accessibility of the ductwork determine the cleaning scope. Filter fit or ventilation components may be relevant when they are part of the reported concern, but do not by themselves establish a need for duct cleaning.

  • Targeted duct and register cleaning
  • Airflow restrictions, crushed ducts, and dampers
  • Filter fit and ventilation components when present and relevant

Assessment and Scope

Questions for the Proposed Work

01

Confirm that observed buildup is the issue

Visible dust or debris in a duct or register can support a targeted cleaning request, but a general dust complaint does not prove the duct network is the source. Inspection should identify the material and its location before defining which sections need attention. This avoids treating cleaning as a standard maintenance interval and helps distinguish duct debris from buildup on other HVAC components or surfaces.

02

Trace weak airflow to its cause

A register with weak airflow may result from debris, but a crushed duct, closed damper, or other obstruction can create a similar symptom. Inspection of the affected run helps determine whether cleaning is appropriate or whether the duct condition needs separate correction. This distinction matters because removing loose material will not restore an air path blocked by damaged ductwork or a closed control.

03

Include related components only when relevant

Where the service request identifies a filter-fit or ventilation concern, those components can be inspected as part of understanding how material enters or moves through the air path. An HRV screen or core is relevant only if that equipment is present and included in the concern. Findings should define any separate cleaning or adjustment needed, rather than treating every component as automatically part of duct cleaning.

Compare the next step

Ask whether poor delivery has been traced to contamination or to another distribution problem. Request evidence of the restriction and a separate explanation of leaks, disconnected branches, or return paths where relevant. Cleaning should address a documented condition rather than substitute for diagnosing why one room receives less conditioned air.

Before You Submit

Equipment Observations to Record

  • Renovation, pest, or water-entry history
  • Duct and filter arrangement

Keep the assessment safe
